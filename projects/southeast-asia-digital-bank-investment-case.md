# An investment case for a bank that could see thin-file customers

The regional opportunity was enormous but not investable by itself. The World Bank’s 2014 Global Findex counted roughly 490 million unbanked adults in East Asia and the Pacific while account ownership had risen from 55% to 69%. Population need did not prove that one digital bank could acquire, verify, underwrite, and retain a specific customer safely.

During my July 2014–June 2016 McKinsey experience, I built the investment and controlled-launch case across customers, a telecom partner, bank product/risk teams, digital distributors, regulators, and the investment committee.

## I made the thesis falsifiable

The intended customers were mobile-first and had little formal credit history. Roughly 70% of the target market was thin-file; the initial approval rate was below 10%.

Capital would be released only if four propositions held together:

- **Utility:** the account solved a frequent payment, saving, or transfer job.
- **Distribution:** telecom and super-app channels could activate qualified users below a $10 CAC target, not the >$100 alternatives.
- **Risk discrimination:** consented behavior improved decisions without using wealth, location, or device proxies as unjustified exclusion.
- **Permission:** identity, consent, explanations, complaints, and monitoring could operate inside a bounded regulatory pilot.

The [BIS–World Bank framework on payment aspects of financial inclusion](https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d144.htm) reinforced the sequencing: a useful transaction account and accessible payments create the foundation. Credit followed usage rather than becoming the only reason to join.

## Customer entry was a ladder, not a binary approval

The underwriting work considered more than 400 signals, including top-up regularity, SIM tenure, usage stability, and observed repayment. I favored consistency over spending level, removed variables acting as unjustified wealth or regional proxies, and flagged sudden pre-application behavior shifts as potential manipulation.

The product offered two routes:

**Sufficient consented history:** enter with a risk-banded starting limit.

**Insufficient history:** receive payments, savings, and a small transaction boundary while building an internal record for 30–60 days.

About 15% of customers used the second route. “No data” therefore meant neither automatic rejection nor uncontrolled credit.

The bank consumed derived behavioral signals rather than unnecessary raw telecom data. Consent had to be explicit and reversible. Electronic identity checks and liveness protected onboarding. Approval, decline reasons, repayment, and losses were monitored by segment so portfolio averages could not hide exclusion or deterioration.

## Capital followed evidence

I joined acquisition and credit in one financial model:

**attributable acquisition spend → completed onboarding → activation → credit eligibility → approval → average exposure → funding need → expected loss/NPL → retained unit economics**

This prevented a growth plan from celebrating low CAC while ignoring credit loss, or a risk plan from constraining approval until distribution no longer worked.

I recommended a bounded launch with named users, safeguards, reporting, and stop conditions before additional capital. The Monetary Authority of Singapore’s June 2016 sandbox consultation is a contemporaneous reference for controlled testing; the record does not establish that this launch occurred in Singapore.

The committee funded milestones:

1. prove identity and consent at onboarding;
2. prove recurring transaction use;
3. release small, risk-banded exposure;
4. observe repayment and segment behavior for a defined window;
5. expand only if acquisition, activation, approval, fairness, and loss gates cleared together.

## Launch evidence

| Capital question | Baseline → target → recorded result | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Could the product reach meaningful active scale? | 0 → 100,000 in 90 days → >100,000 in Q1 | Program active-user definition; exact activity window not retained |
| Could thin-file access expand responsibly? | <10% approval → >40% inside loss gate → >40% | Approved completed eligible applications / completed eligible applications |
| Could distribution avoid subsidy economics? | alternatives >$100 CAC → <$10 → <$10 | Attributable acquisition spend / activated customers |
| Did early credit quality remain investable? | new portfolio → within launch boundary → 2.4% NPL | Non-performing balance / outstanding launch-cohort balance; seasoning window not retained |
| Did no-history customers receive a controlled route? | none → create 30–60-day evidence path → ~15% used it | Customers entering limited route / onboarded eligible customers |

Because the active-user window and loan seasoning are absent, I do not imply more precision than the record supports. The important result was concurrent: access, acquisition cost, and early credit quality all cleared the launch boundaries.

My committee responsibility covered the falsifiable thesis, customer-entry architecture, signal principles, joined acquisition/credit economics, staged capital release, gates, and launch measurement. Product designed the service; Risk set model/portfolio limits; the telco supplied data/distribution; regulators and counsel determined permission.

The investment case held because no single “alternative-data model” carried it. A useful transaction account created behavior, embedded distribution controlled CAC, graduated limits constrained exposure, consent made data usable, and segment monitoring preserved decision quality. Inclusion became a fundable operating system, not a headline approval rate.
