## Evidence-grounded premise

NIST's GenAI Profile frames risks across governance, data, models, evaluation, and use. NIST CSF 2.0 adds governance and supply-chain cybersecurity outcomes. Together they support acquisition diligence that evaluates product differentiation alongside model dependency, data rights, control maturity, security, and integration risk.

## Case approach

- Compare build, partner, minority investment, and acquisition against the same strategic capabilities.
- Diligence model provenance, data rights, evaluation quality, tool permissions, security, and customer concentration.
- Value reusable technology and distribution separately from uncertain agent autonomy claims.
- Define integration gates for identity, policy, telemetry, incident response, and customer migration before closing.

## Evidence-based success measures

Use verified capability coverage, unresolved rights issues, security findings, integration dependencies, retention risk, and value sensitivity. These are proposed measures, not historical results.
