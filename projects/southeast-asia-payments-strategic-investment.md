# Southeast Asia Payments — QR Infrastructure Investment Strategy

> **Portfolio lens:** Strategic minority investment, market and regulatory diligence, product integration, corridor sequencing, and ownership rights.

## Executive snapshot

Redirected a $50M consumer-wallet thesis toward the scarce infrastructure connecting regional wallets, banks, and QR schemes. A 15% minority investment combined sticky B2B economics with strategic payment integration and a corridor recovery plan after the original regulatory timeline proved too optimistic.

## Resume-ready impact

- Reframed a $50M fintech thesis from subsidized consumer wallets toward interoperable QR infrastructure with 1.5–2.0% take rates and embedded switching costs.
- Supported a 15% strategic investment and integration that connected an existing payment product to more than five million QR acceptance points.
- Recovered from a 12-month licensing delay by resequencing Thailand and Singapore; the B2B portfolio reached breakeven in 14 months and held roughly 1.5x book value.

## Interview story

### Situation

Consumer-wallet CAC had tripled, unsubsidized retention was below 40%, and take rates approached 0.5%. The investment committee favored visible brands, while the infrastructure layer offered stronger economics and strategic utility.

### Task

Build an investable thesis, validate commercial and technical fit, and translate a minority position into measurable product and distribution value.

### Actions

- Mapped market fragmentation, take rates, licensing, switching costs, and existing customer demand across corridors.
- Connected the minority investment to a modern-API bridge for a legacy ISO 8583 payments core.
- Documented the Vietnam timing miss, re-scored corridors, and launched clearer pathways first.
- Used owned travel signals to activate existing customers rather than acquire local consumers.

### Results

- More than five million QR acceptance points became available across selected markets.
- The B2B portfolio reached EBITDA breakeven in 14 months.
- The infrastructure portfolio held about 1.5x book value.
- The regulatory miss produced a stronger corridor-level diligence and spending-gate model.

## Decisions and trade-offs

- Back the interoperability layer rather than another subsidized wallet.
- Separate company-level investment merit from corridor-level launch readiness.
- Preserve ownership optionality through pro-rata rights or warrants in future transactions.

## Leadership signal

Aligned investment, product, engineering, board, and regional regulatory stakeholders around both financial return and strategic integration value.
