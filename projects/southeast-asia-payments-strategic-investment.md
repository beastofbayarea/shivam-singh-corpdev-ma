# Southeast Asia Payments — QR Infrastructure Investment Strategy

## What I worked on

I completed this work during my [Rakuten experience from June to December 2023](https://github.com/beastofbayarea/shivam-singh-corpdev-ma/blob/main/shivam-singh-corpdev-ma.pdf).

I redirected a $50M consumer-wallet thesis toward the scarce infrastructure connecting regional wallets, banks, and QR schemes. A 15% minority investment combined sticky B2B economics with strategic payment integration and a corridor recovery plan after the original regulatory timeline proved too optimistic.

## At a glance

- I reframed a $50M fintech thesis from subsidized consumer wallets toward interoperable QR infrastructure with 1.5–2.0% take rates and embedded switching costs.
- I supported a 15% strategic investment and integration that connected an existing payment product to more than five million QR acceptance points.
- I recovered from a 12-month licensing delay by resequencing Thailand and Singapore; the B2B portfolio reached breakeven in 14 months and held roughly 1.5x book value.

## The situation

Consumer-wallet CAC had tripled, unsubsidized retention was below 40%, and take rates approached 0.5%. The investment committee favored visible brands, while the infrastructure layer offered stronger economics and strategic utility.

## What I needed to accomplish

I needed to build an investable thesis, validate commercial and technical fit, and translate a minority position into measurable product and distribution value.

## What I did

- I mapped market fragmentation, take rates, licensing, switching costs, and existing customer demand across corridors.
- I connected the minority investment to a modern-API bridge for a legacy ISO 8583 payments core.
- I documented the Vietnam timing miss, re-scored corridors, and launched clearer pathways first.
- I used owned travel signals to activate existing customers rather than acquire local consumers.

## The results

- More than five million QR acceptance points became available across selected markets.
- The B2B portfolio reached EBITDA breakeven in 14 months.
- The infrastructure portfolio held about 1.5x book value.
- The regulatory miss produced a stronger corridor-level diligence and spending-gate model.

## Decisions and trade-offs

- I backed the interoperability layer rather than another subsidized wallet.
- I separated company-level investment merit from corridor-level launch readiness.
- I preserved ownership optionality through pro-rata rights or warrants in future transactions.

## How I led

I aligned investment, product, engineering, board, and regional regulatory stakeholders around both financial return and strategic integration value.

## A note on measurement

I treat the Vietnam licensing assumption as a documented miss. I used that lesson to require independent regulatory validation and staged integration spending for future corridors.

## Why I chose this approach

I used [World Bank - Fast Payments Toolkit (2021)](https://fastpayments.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/Fast%20Payment%20Flagship_Final_Nov%201.pdf) to ground market and implementation framework for fast payments, QR, APIs, risk, and interoperability. I used [PCI SSC - PCI DSS 4.0 release (2022)](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/about_us/press_releases/securing-the-future-of-payments-pci-ssc-publishes-pci-data-security-standard-v4-0/) to ground payment-data security and validation standard.
